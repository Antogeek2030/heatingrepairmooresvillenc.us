Cooler weather arrives gradually around Lake Norman, but waiting until the first cold snap to discover a heating problem is avoidable. A little preparation helps systems start the season reliably.

## Schedule a Tune-Up

A fall maintenance visit typically includes cleaning, inspection of safety controls, and a performance check. Catching a weak ignitor, dirty flame sensor, or restricted airflow early is far less disruptive than a no-heat call on a freezing morning.

## Replace the Filter

Start the season with a clean filter and set a reminder for regular changes. Restricted airflow is one of the most common contributors to short cycling and limit trips.

## Test the System Before You Need It

Turn the heat on for a full cycle on a mild day. Listen for unusual noises, confirm warm air at the registers, and note any odd smells (a light dust odor on first startup is common; a persistent gas or burning smell is not).

## Check Vents and Returns

Make sure supply registers and return grilles are open and not blocked by furniture or rugs. Closed vents can increase system pressure and reduce comfort in other rooms.

## Know Your Shutoffs

Locate the furnace power switch, the breaker, and—for gas systems—the gas shutoff. Knowing where these are saves time if service is needed.

Taking care of these basics does not guarantee a problem-free winter, but it does improve the odds. If you would like help preparing your system, [reach out](/contact/) to schedule a maintenance visit.
