A furnace that will not start is stressful, especially when temperatures drop. Before assuming the worst, a few simple checks can identify easy fixes—or confirm that professional service is needed.

## Thermostat Settings

Confirm the thermostat is set to heat and the setpoint is several degrees above the current room temperature. If you have a programmable or smart thermostat, check that a schedule or hold setting is not overriding your intent.

## Power and Breakers

Furnaces need power for the control board, inducer, and blower. Check the dedicated breaker and any switch near the furnace. A tripped breaker or accidentally switched-off disconnect is a common oversight.

## Air Filter

A severely clogged filter can cause the furnace to overheat and shut down on a safety limit. If the filter looks packed with dust, replace it and reset the system.

## Condensate and Safety Switches

High-efficiency furnaces produce condensate. A clogged drain or full condensate pump can open a safety switch and prevent operation. Clear visible clogs if you are comfortable doing so, or note the issue for the technician.

## When to Call

If basic checks do not restore heat, or if you smell gas, hear unusual noises, or see error codes you cannot interpret, professional diagnosis is the next step. Fuel-burning equipment should be serviced by someone familiar with combustion and venting safety.
